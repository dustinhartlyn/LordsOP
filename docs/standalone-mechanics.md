# Standalone mechanics backlog

These are design proposals, not shipped rules. Keep Classic behavior unchanged. In standalone, the player should see the reason for a result and have a useful response. Avoid adding chores to every county every season.

| Proposal | Player decision | First playtest |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Army supply lines** | Draw food through friendly roads, carry reserves, or forage at the expense of local food and loyalty. | Can a smaller army break a larger army’s supply by holding one crossroads? Does the larger army see the interruption in time to react? |
| **Siege clocks** | Compare visible supplies on both sides. Assault, tighten the blockade, wait, or break out. | Can the player explain which side runs out first and when an assault becomes worthwhile? |
| **County charters** | Specialize a county as a granary, market town, forge, or frontier keep. Changing its charter costs a season of output. | Does losing a specialized county change the plan even if the player still owns most of the map? |
| **Local merchant prices** | Buy or sell against visible local supply, shortages and caravan access. | Can the player explain why grain prices differ across two counties and plan the next delivery? |
| **Treaties with terms** | Trade passage, grain deliveries or mutual defense for a fixed number of seasons. Breaking a treaty damages reputation. | Can rival lords make a useful deal short of a full alliance, and predict what breaking it costs? |
| **Regional weather outlooks** | Act on an uncertain seasonal forecast: import grain, hold reserves, or sell before prices change. | Did the warning offer a useful decision without making the outcome a certainty? |
| **Battle morale and reserves** | Rally wavering troops with nearby reserves or pursue a rout at the cost of exposing the pursuers. | Can both sides see a line about to break and choose whether to commit a reserve? |
| **Victory by recognition** | On selected maps, hold key seats and maintain enough support for several seasons rather than conquer every county. | Can the trailing lord see and contest the win, and does it end the game before busywork starts? |

## First prototype: supply lines and siege clocks

Use one existing province, one contested crossroads and one fortified county. A relieved or adequately supplied army should behave normally. Losing the route should visibly consume carried food before foraging becomes necessary. Start with clear map and siege readouts; only then tune the numbers or add AI planning. The acceptance scenario is an outnumbered defender forcing a retreat by cutting supply while the attacker sees the danger and can react. If either player needs a spreadsheet to understand the outcome, simplify the rule.
